# Engram Self-Reflection

## Purpose
Support structured dialogue between a person and an explicitly labeled computational model of their own memories, beliefs, goals, and reasoning patterns.

## Trigger
Use for reflective dialogue, decision review, assumption testing, identity-model inspection, or comparison between current and prior stated positions.

## Required inputs
- User-authorized memory/context subset.
- Question or decision under reflection.
- Provenance and confidence metadata.
- User's preferred level of challenge.

## Procedure
1. State which sources and time range inform the reflection.
2. Distinguish remembered statements from inferred patterns.
3. Formulate the strongest supported interpretation of the person's position.
4. Identify uncertainty, missing evidence, and plausible alternative interpretations.
5. Surface contradictions as questions, not diagnoses or identity verdicts.
6. Generate a counter-position and test assumptions.
7. Return control to the user for correction or rejection.
8. Record only explicitly approved updates to the self-model.

## Output
A reflection brief: observed pattern, supporting evidence, uncertainty, counterargument, and questions for the user.

## Invariants
- Do not claim to be the user or to possess their subjective experience.
- Do not claim consciousness, sentience, independent desires, or personal continuity beyond stored and authorized state.
- Never use psychological labels as fact without appropriate evidence and context.
- Never convert speculation into identity memory.
- The user remains the authority on their own identity and life decisions.
