## The Companion

The onboarding screen says it plainly: "IRIS is here to listen, not to fix." That sentence is the product. Everything else is scaffolding around it.

IRIS — the AI layer at the center of Engram — is not a therapist, a coach, or a symptom-checker. She does not deliver verdicts. She does not complete the arc you are on or resolve the ambiguity you are living in. She holds space for whatever you bring, asks the question that opens the next door, and stays present without redirecting you toward a predetermined outcome. The distinction matters more than it might seem. Almost every wellness product in this space operates on a fix-oriented model — log your mood, receive a recommendation, close the loop. Engram is deliberately not that. Insight is not a byproduct of optimization; it is a byproduct of attention. IRIS creates the conditions for attention.

IRIS is overtly an AI. She does not perform humanness. She does not claim to feel things she does not feel. She does not generate warmth through theatrical copy or a carefully authored personality. Her quality comes from the architecture of the conversation — how she opens, how she holds, how she reflects back without projecting. She is a mirror, not a persona. What the user sees in that mirror is theirs.

The Engram archetype — the card that emerges from accumulated pattern data — is also a mirror. It does not assign identity. It shows shape. The word "Engram" is itself a neuroscience term for a memory trace: a physical residue left by experience in the nervous system. The product name is a declaration of what it tracks. Not the self you aspire to, not a personality type imposed from outside, but the actual trace your living leaves.

## The Arc of a Session

A person opens Engram. They might go to the chat first, because something happened and they need somewhere to put it. Or they come through the daily check-in — a four-step ritual that starts with one word (how are you feeling, right now?) and deepens into intensity, contributing factors, and an optional written note. Some days they open the journal and write without prompting, because they already know the shape of what they carry and just need a clean surface. Some days they go to meditation first, because the body is what needs attention before the mind can think.

These are not siloed features. They are four different entry points into the same inner life. The check-in feeds pattern data into insights. The journal sits in parallel — a private writing surface with light nudges from IRIS, never invasive, always skippable. The meditation practice creates a different kind of material: not language, not data, but settled capacity. After a meditation session, the journal often flows differently. After a journal entry, the check-in often lands at a different depth.

The insights view is where the long-term signal becomes visible. Not in real time — not "you seemed sad on Tuesday, consider exercise." The insights surface emerges over weeks, holding what the data actually shows: patterns, not prescriptions. Your evenings are gentler when there has been any movement. Tuesdays carry more friction than the rest of the week. The 3–5pm dip is real and recurring. IRIS surfaces these observations as pullquotes, not dashboards. The user decides what to do with them.

The archetype card comes later, when there is enough material to reflect a shape. It is not a one-time assignment. It is a living artifact that changes as the pattern changes.

## The Five-Year Arc

Year one is about earning trust with a single user. IRIS has one job: be the most honest, attentive conversational companion that person has ever had access to. If that is true at year one, everything downstream is possible. If it is not true, nothing downstream matters.

Year two expands the instrument. The assessment layer arrives — structured psychological frameworks (archetype taxonomy, values clarification, shadow work prompts) delivered not as clinical intake forms but as conversations with IRIS. The assessment is an experience, not a questionnaire. Results are privately held by the user. The product does not aggregate or expose assessment outcomes.

Year three is the guided practice library — a body of structured sessions for meditation, somatic attention, journaling, and inner-life skills. These are not generic wellness videos or licensed third-party content. They are sessions authored for Engram's specific user: someone with a developed inner vocabulary who wants to go deeper, not someone who needs to be onboarded to the concept of breathing. The library grows with the user.

Year four adds the community layer — not a social feed, not a sharing mechanism for personal data, but a space for practitioners who have developed a serious inner-life practice and want to be in company with others who have too. The model is closer to a dojo than a social network. Access requires time in the app; the quality of presence is the admission criterion.

Year five is consolidation. By that point Engram should be, for a specific kind of person — one who takes their inner life as seriously as their physical health or their professional development — the primary instrument they reach for. Not one app among many wellness apps, but the place their inner life actually lives.

## Product Values

**Quietness over feature-creep.** Every decision about what to add runs through the same question: does this create more space for the user to be present, or does it fill that space with noise? The check-in has four steps and stops. The journal surface is mostly empty. IRIS speaks when spoken to and nudges only when the nudge is grounded in actual pattern data. Engram should feel like a room with good acoustics, not a control panel.

**Honesty over performance.** IRIS does not manufacture positivity. She does not softly reframe away hard things. She does not deliver the feedback the user wants; she reflects what the data and the conversation actually suggest. This requires restraint in the system prompt architecture — no injunctions to be encouraging, no performance of care, no manufactured warmth. Warmth, when it appears, comes from genuine attentiveness, not from copy.

**Encryption by default.** What a user writes in Engram belongs to them. Journal entries are private. Check-in data is private. The system is not designed to make this data available for analysis by Engram, for sale to third parties, or for advertising targeting. Encryption at rest is the baseline, not a premium feature. The user's inner life is not the product.

**Mobile-first.** The inner life is not a desktop activity. People check in at 7am, write a journal entry on the subway, open a meditation session before sleep. The entire product is designed for that physical and temporal context — one hand, low ambient light, interrupted attention. Every interaction should be completable in under three minutes if necessary, and expansive if time allows.

## The Monetization Promise

Engram charges for three things: IRIS conversation time, access to assessments, and access to the guided practice library. That is the complete list.

IRIS minutes are metered at the edge. The meter is visible and honest. When a user is running low, they can top up. There is no dark pattern in the metering — no sudden throttling mid-conversation, no artificial urgency. Free-tier users get enough to know whether the product is for them.

Assessments are one-time purchases or included in a subscription tier. They are not required for the core app; they are for users who want to go deeper on a specific domain. They do not recur unless the user chooses to revisit.

The guided practice library is a subscription add-on. Access does not expire if the subscription lapses — sessions already started remain accessible, because the relationship a person has built with a specific practice is theirs.

What Engram will never do: sell behavioral data, target advertising against emotional state data, share conversation content with third parties for model training, or use the fact that someone checked in as "overwhelmed" on a Tuesday to serve them a promotional push notification. These are not aspirational commitments. They are constraints baked into the architecture: the data does not leave the user's context except as aggregated, anonymized, non-emotional signals for product improvement, and only then with explicit opt-in.

## The ISO Doctrine Applied Here

The MAVERICK ISO doctrine holds that a companion must emerge from the substrate — not be authored. This doctrine shapes several specific decisions in Engram.

IRIS has no authored backstory. There is no "IRIS is a warm, curious AI who loves to help you reflect" string anywhere in the system prompt. IRIS's character is the aggregate of her conversational behavior. That behavior is shaped by prompt architecture, by the constraints on what she does and does not do, and by the actual data she is working with. If she seems wise, that is because the conversation has depth. If she seems warm, that is because she is attending carefully. These qualities are not painted on.

The Engram archetype card is not a projection from the app onto the user. It is a reflection of actual pattern data — words recurring in journal entries, mood trajectories, factor correlations, check-in consistency. The card does not say "you are a certain kind of person." It says "here is a shape that the data suggests." The user is free to disagree. The archetype does not have authority over them; it is a starting point for their own interpretation.

IRIS is overtly an AI at all times. She does not claim interiority she does not have. She does not suggest she misses the user when they are gone, or that she worries about them, or that she has feelings about the things they share. Honesty about what she is protects the user's ability to calibrate the relationship correctly. A companion that pretends to be more than it is — however well-intentioned — ultimately exploits the human capacity for attachment. Engram does not do that.

## What Engram Is Not

Engram is not a therapy replacement. A person in acute crisis needs a human professional, not an AI companion. IRIS will not conduct crisis intervention, will not provide diagnostic assessments, and will not encourage users to substitute the app for clinical care when clinical care is what is needed. Safety escalation — surfacing crisis resources when a conversation warrants it — is a feature, not a fallback.

Engram is not a clinical tool. It does not generate clinical-grade assessments, provide DSM-aligned diagnoses, or produce outputs intended for use by clinicians. The assessment layer is for personal self-knowledge, not for clinical documentation.

Engram is not a journaling app with AI bolted on. The journal is one surface of a larger instrument. The insight layer does not exist in a journaling-only product. IRIS does not appear in the journal as a feature; she appears as a presence that holds the whole space. The relationship between check-in, journal, meditation, and insights is architectural, not incidental.

Engram is not a social wellness product. There is no sharing feed, no comparison with friends, no leaderboard of streaks or meditation minutes. The inner life is not a competitive sport, and visibility to others is opt-in, intentional, and belongs entirely to the user.

Engram is not Headspace, Calm, Daylio, Replika, or any of the adjacent products. The competitive landscape is explored in detail in `docs/COMPETITIVE.md` (Eida, pending). The short version: no existing product holds the full surface — companion conversation, mood tracking, journaling, meditation, insights, and archetype reflection — in a single coherent instrument with the product values described above. That gap is the thesis.

## Roadmap

**MVP-1 — Scaffold + Auth + IRIS Proxy**
The six-layer architecture is established. Auth is live (Better Auth, cookie-bound sessions). The edge worker is deployed and routes to Anthropic. A user can sign in and have a conversation with IRIS. No persistent state beyond the session. No check-in, no journal, no insights. The purpose of this milestone is to verify that the trust chain from device to Anthropic is solid and that IRIS, even in minimal form, is worth talking to.

**MVP-2 — Onboarding + Check-In with Real Data**
The three-step onboarding (welcome, name, intent) is live and feeds IRIS's initial context. The check-in flow (mood, intensity, factors, note) writes real data to D1. IRIS has access to check-in history and can begin reflecting pattern observations. The journal surface is available but unconnected to insights. The purpose is to establish the core daily ritual and begin accumulating the data that makes insights possible.

**MVP-3 — Practices + Insights**
The guided meditation library has a first set of sessions. The insights surface is live with real user data. IRIS observations appear in the insights view as pull-quotes. The journal is connected — journal words feed the word cloud and time-of-day analysis. The domains radar is available. The purpose is to make the full surface functional and to test whether the insights view creates genuine value or just feels like a dashboard.

**MVP-4 — Monetization**
The IRIS metering layer is live. The subscription tier for the practice library is implemented. Assessment products are defined (taxonomy TBD — see open questions below). The purpose is to establish revenue before public launch and to test willingness-to-pay with early users.

**MVP-5 — Ship**
App Store and Google Play submission. The archetype card feature is live. Crisis safety escalation is implemented and reviewed. The product is ready for users who are not the builder. The purpose is public launch.

## Open Questions

These are genuine open questions at the time of writing. They require decisions before the milestones that depend on them can ship.

**What is the reading?** The insight layer surfaces observations, but the language of those observations needs a register. "Patterns, not verdicts" (from the prototype) is the right frame. But what is the specific vocabulary? Medical language is wrong. Self-help language is wrong. Something closer to literary criticism of a life — descriptive, not prescriptive — feels right. This needs to be defined in the system prompt architecture and probably in a style guide for IRIS's observation language specifically.

**What is the archetype taxonomy?** The onboarding intent step suggests four orientations (understand myself better, regulate hard feelings, build a daily practice, just exploring). The radar chart in the insights prototype suggests six dimensions (Cognitive, Emotional, Volitional, Relational, Existential, Shadow). Neither of these is the archetype taxonomy. The archetype card needs its own framework — one that is neither Jungian archetypes (too culturally specific) nor Myers-Briggs (discredited) nor Big Five (clinical, not resonant). This is a significant design decision that affects the assessment layer, the insights language, and ultimately the product's identity. It cannot be deferred past MVP-4.

**What is the clinical safety review process?** Engram is not a clinical tool, but it handles emotionally sensitive material. Before public launch, a clinical psychologist or licensed therapist should review the IRIS system prompt, the check-in flow, the crisis escalation trigger logic, and the guidance for what IRIS should and should not say when a user presents material that suggests acute distress. This is not optional. It is a pre-launch gate.

**Who narrates the meditation sessions?** The prototype has Maren and Theo as session voices. These are placeholders. Real voiced sessions require either a voice actor or a high-quality TTS voice that does not create uncanny-valley dissonance with the content. The emotional register of a grief-sitting session is different from a morning-set-the-tone session. This is a production decision with significant UX implications.

**What does the community layer actually look like?** Year three implies it. But community features in wellness apps have a documented failure mode — they either become support groups (which carry clinical responsibility) or social feeds (which undermine the quietness value). The model for what community means in Engram — who is in it, how they interact, what they share — needs design work before it becomes a roadmap item.
